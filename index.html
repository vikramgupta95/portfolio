A Behavioral Analysis of Online Shopping — Mumbai City

Bachelor's research project, guided by Pawan Gupta (Senior Analyst) — Amity University Online

Methodology

Used a descriptive research design with random sampling to study online shopping behavior among Mumbai consumers. Built a structured questionnaire covering demographics, platform preference, purchase drivers, and pain points, then collected 100 valid responses through personal interviews.

80%Prefer online over offline
31% / 31%Trust Amazon / Flipkart equally
37.5%Pay via Cash on Delivery
50%Of shoppers are students
Key findings
  • Convenience, time savings, product variety, and discounts came out as the strongest drivers of online purchase decisions.
  • Awareness spreads mainly through advertisements (33%) and word-of-mouth from friends (31%) — word-of-mouth matters almost as much as paid ads.
  • Return complications, fear of payment fraud, and the inability to physically inspect products remain the biggest barriers to trust.
Recommendations delivered

Translated findings into 7 actionable recommendations — including simplifying return & refund policies, building digital payment trust through secure gateways, and running student-targeted campaigns, since students made up half the respondent base.
